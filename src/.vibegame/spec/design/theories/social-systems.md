# Social Systems and Resource Management

Apply sociological theories to design game social interactions and shared resource management.

---

## 1. Dunbar's Number and Social Networks

### Theoretical Basis

Any person's social network consists of approximately 150 stable social relationships — the upper limit of people one can know and benefit from.

### Design Application

Leverage network externalities: Maximize benefits from each individual in the social network (mutual help, access, etc.). Build positive feedback loops to enhance game influence, increase stickiness, reduce churn. Leverage "fear of missing out" psychology.

---

## 2. Social Mechanism Types

### Active Social
- **Competition**: Leaderboards
- **Cooperation**: Gift exchange

### Passive Social
- **Display**: Friend lists

### Design Considerations

- Evaluate whether the design complements friend requests, rating prompts, gift loops, high score competition, asynchronous races, traditional PvP or cooperative modes
- Consider target audience preferences (some players strongly prefer cooperation over competition)
- Note legal and privacy issues (e.g., chat features for children under 13)
- Clever use of social design can increase game complexity and entertainment value

## 3. Tragedy of the Commons and Shared Resources

### Definition

There exist finite, shared resources available to everyone that will eventually be depleted, harming everyone. Individual rationality leads to collective irrationality.

### Trigger Conditions

Resource usage growth within a finite system — user population grows, resources do not.

### Player Strategies

- Fixed-order games: First movers may grab what they can
- Simultaneous games: May lead to everyone "grabbing while they can"

### Design Prevention Measures

1. **Information transparency**: Provide information about resource overuse, raise conservation awareness
2. **Sense of belonging**: Ensure strong community belonging, reduce selfish hoarding
3. **Institutional management**: Establish trustworthy institutions to manage key resource allocation
4. **Incentives and penalties**: Incentivize responsible use, penalize overuse

### Notes

- Tragedy of the commons solutions are complex, with no ideal method
- Not based on specific economic/political systems or corporate greed
- Equal resource redistribution: Those who can't use efficiently still receive, leading to waste
- Monopolized resources: Efficiency far lower than corruption and regulatory seizure

---

## Design Application

Design around interesting, difficult choices, giving players more challenge and exploration space. Protocols or special rules are needed to reward players who use fewer resources.
